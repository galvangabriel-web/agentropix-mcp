00 Bottom line up front

A confirmed, hands-on-keyboard intrusion sat inside the shieldbase.lan environment for roughly 143 days (≈ 4.7 months) before the captured evidence window closed. The attacker entered through an internet-facing file-transfer server, went quiet for over a month, then moved laterally to the domain controller and stood up durable, automated persistence. By the final captured day the operation was running a disciplined, machine-grade command-and-control beacon — the signature of a capable, organized adversary, not opportunistic malware.

This is a domain-controller compromise. The blast radius is the entire Windows identity fabric. We must treat every credential in the domain as exposed until the recovery actions below are complete.

03 Critical & High Findings

The executive tier surfaces every critical and high finding (6 of the 9), each as one back-anchored line. Card title, severity, and the business-impact sentence are projected verbatim from the analyst finding; the [A#] proof link resolves into analyst.md (no-drift). Plain-language gloss is added for executive reading. Medium findings (F-07/08/09) are counted in the KPIs but not shown here.

F-01 Domain controller fully compromised — bi-daily persistence + terminal C2 critical

The attacker installed scheduled tasks on the domain controller that re-ran twice a day for four months (May–Sep 2018), rotating the schedule three times to stay hidden. The DC is the master key to every login in the company.

Business impact  Catastrophic  Control of the DC means the attacker could potentially authenticate as any user or service. Trust in every credential is broken until recovery completes.
Downtime / regulatory  Identity recovery requires a controlled, scheduled disruption. Breach-notification obligations may be triggered — legal should be engaged now.
Likelihood: highly_likely · Confidence: high · Proof: [A1] analyst.md#dc-persistence

F-02 Pass-the-hash domain traversal across the DMZ→internal boundary critical

Sub-100ms EID 4648+4672 credential-replay clusters from MICROSO-8N79483 and base-ftp drove PsExec/Impacket service installs into the internal zone. In plain terms: the attacker logged in with stolen password hashes and rode a single perimeter foothold all the way to the domain crown jewels — because explicit-credential auth was allowed to cross the DMZ→internal boundary.

Business impact  Catastrophic for identity  Any replayed credential is a standing re-entry key; the flat DMZ→internal trust converts one FTP foothold into domain compromise.
Likelihood: highly_likely · Confidence: high · Proof: [A2] analyst.md#pass-the-hash

F-04 Six persistent backdoor local accounts on dmz-ftp high

The attacker created 6 local backdoor accounts on the DMZ FTP host across 4 different dates, adding several to privileged groups. These are independent re-entry points that survive a single password change.

Business impact  High  Even after we close the front door, these accounts let the attacker walk back in. They must be removed before any all-clear.
Downtime  Removal is low-disruption but time-critical — it is a P0 action.
Likelihood: highly_likely · Confidence: high · Proof: [A3] analyst.md#backdoor-accounts

F-06 Credential harvesting in DC memory high

Memory carving recovered findstr.exe×2 on the DC, parented to the orphaned command shells — searching files and registry exports for stored secrets. In plain terms: the attacker hunted for passwords directly inside the domain controller's memory.

Business impact  High  Any secret recovered from the DC is a re-entry key; reinforces the assume-all-credentials-exposed posture and the krbtgt double-reset.
Likelihood: likely · Confidence: moderate · Proof: [A6] analyst.md#cred-harvest

F-03 Active C2 channel live at the edge of collection critical

On the final captured day (2018-09-07), the host was beaconing out every 36 seconds with no jitter in two waves — the disciplined, machine-timed pattern of a commercial offensive toolkit (Cobalt Strike / Meterpreter-class). The last recorded event was 21:23:15 UTC.

Business impact  High  Indicates an active, capable operator with a live channel out of our network — not dormant malware.
Trust framing  Professional tooling implies a deliberate, resourced adversary; assume intent to exfiltrate or extort.
Likelihood: highly_likely · Confidence: moderate · Proof: [A3] analyst.md#c2-beacon

F-05 Scheduled-task / service persistence on the DC (3 rotations) high

The bi-daily service-install cadence on the DC resolves to 3 schedule rotations across May–Sep 2018 — the attacker changed the task definition three times to dodge static detection while keeping a re-entry mechanism that works even if the beacon is killed. In plain terms: a second, independent way back in, hidden on the domain controller.

Business impact  High  Durable re-entry independent of the C2 beacon; killing the beacon alone does not evict the actor. Presence beyond the DC is inferred, not yet enumerated — so the hunt is fleet-wide.
Likelihood: highly_likely · Confidence: high · Proof: [A5] analyst.md#sched-task-persistence

07 How to read the two ratings (legend)

Likelihood — FIRST 5-tier (did it happen?)

The engine carries the FIRST 5-tier scale as a closed Literal — these are the exact tokens that appear in the analyst tier and the no-drift back-anchors:

Term (engine Literal)Ordinal weightReads as
almost_certain5near-certain the activity occurred
highly_likely4strong, corroborated evidence
likely3probable; some corroboration
unlikely2weak / contested signal
remote1improbable on current evidence

Ordinal weight feeds the Business tier risk score (likelihood_weight x severity_impact_weight, 0..25). Likelihood is the same scale the analyst tier prints per finding.

Confidence — LCA (how good is our evidence?): High / Moderate / Low. Reflects source reliability, corroboration, and analytic rigor — reported separately from likelihood and never combined into a single number.
